# UX rationale

## Research question first

The user begins with a scientific question, not a numerical-software menu. The interface then exposes the authored model, evidence, limitations, and capability gaps rather than hiding them behind a magical answer.

## Credential before model

A provider key may be required to discover which models the account can access. The settings workflow therefore saves the credential first and treats model selection as a separate operation. The dropdown is populated from the provider account rather than a permanently frozen list.

## Chat and evidence coexist

The research drawer remains visible beside the laboratory. It can occupy 10% to 60% of the workspace and can collapse without destroying drafts or conversation state. Research-world history, source attachments, branching, cancellation, and message reuse support longer investigative work rather than one-shot prompts.

## Shared molecular world

The agent and the human view the same normalized molecular record. The human receives a 3D rendering; the agent receives structured diagnostics, ligand groups, warnings, and bounded atom/bond context. Neither channel is treated as sufficient proof.

## Progressive disclosure

Basic users can import a structure and inspect warnings. More advanced controls reveal QM/MM-region design, engine readiness, charge, multiplicity, residue boundaries, and evidence-gated campaign stages.

## Provenance over magic

Every durable artifact identifies its research world, source, time, method boundary, and limitations. Missing engines remain visibly missing.
