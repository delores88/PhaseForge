## What the diagnostics can establish

The public [constraint/history implementation](https://github.com/IAS-Astrophysics/athenak/blob/c5a0d7f9155a70149931bf0be5a4ffb673f2532a/src/outputs/history.cpp) emits proper-volume integrals of squared constraints over cells passing the `chi>=0.0625` mask. Calculate `sqrt(H_norm2/Volume)` and `sqrt(M_norm2/Volume)` and preserve mask, volume and native fields. Those are not already normalized RMS values. Finite constraints alone do not establish converged physical accuracy.

Three explicit public [FastFlow](https://github.com/IAS-Astrophysics/athenak/blob/c5a0d7f9155a70149931bf0be5a4ffb673f2532a/src/z4c/fastflow.cpp) searches cover each puncture and a common surface centered at zero; start/stop times are explicit for all three. The input declares individual starting radii 0.3 and common radius 4; the later independent source audit established that tracked individual searches override those input radii with tracker masses, giving actual first guesses 0.5. The common guess remains 4. Other settings are `ntheta=10`, `lmax=6`, at most 100 flow iterations, mass-change tolerance `1e-6`. Per-search verbose output, grid shapes and harmonic coefficients are retained. These coarse diagnostic settings do not establish accurate horizon properties.

Material source caveats: `ah_found` tests successive irreducible-mass change, not a small expansion residual. `hmean_tol` is an abort ceiling. The field named `hrms` is the area average of expansion **squared**, without the square root; `hmean` is an area integral. Failed searches can print stale previously successful properties. Therefore a reported common horizon requires current successful-search evidence, small independently assessed expansion residual, a surface enclosing both current punctures and spatial-resolution checks; a finite summary row or a coordinate-separation trigger is insufficient. No common horizon is expected or promised by coordinate time one at the proposed separation.

Finite-radius waveform extraction is enabled at radii 8 and 10, geodesic resolution five (252 angular samples each), cadence 0.25. The public output is **rPsi4**, including 77 complex harmonic modes for `l=2…8`, not strain. This short, small-domain pilot tests output wiring only. Physical radiation requires a larger domain, longer retarded-time window, junk removal and radius/resolution convergence. An x-axis collision is not purely `m=0` in the code's z-polar harmonic basis.

## Required target remains unresolved

Bowen–York data solve a constrained ansatz; existence of a numerical solution at a large input momentum does not establish a specified physical boost. The tracker reports coordinate motion from minus the shift, not invariant speed. The requested `v/c=0.999` corresponds to `gamma=22.366272` only after the frame/rest-mass convention is fixed. It requires individual horizon masses/spins, total ADM energy/momentum, separation/binding and junk-radiation treatment, physical-boost calibration, and a stable validated high-boost evolution. The public pinned moving-puncture gauge and this pilot do not supply that validation. Previous AthenaK high-boost results around gamma 5.1 do not validate extrapolation to gamma 22.366. A lower-momentum run, even if it later produces a credible common horizon, remains a prerequisite rather than fulfillment.
