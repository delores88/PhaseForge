## What is publicly implementable for the requested boost

The target convention requires `gamma=22.3662720421` and asymptotic `P/(m c)=22.3439057701`. The pilot's Bowen–York parameters `P=±0.025` and target end masses 0.5 do not establish physical velocity. Coordinate puncture motion is gauge dependent.

**Bowen–York cannot be dismissed by an old universal speed ceiling.** Zhu, Pretorius and Stone explicitly calibrate Bowen–York data using separation-dependent ADM energy, junk subtraction and mass diagnostics. Their Appendix A fits `E_ADM(d)=2 gamma0 M0 + E_junk + C1/d + C2/d²`; it also distinguishes finite-separation effects. Their reported evolved maximum is approximately gamma 5.1, with a new lapse driver. The evidence neither validates gamma 22.366 nor proves that Bowen–York cannot reach it. In particular, extending their junk estimate or assuming negligible horizon absorption at the target would be untested extrapolation. This review rechecked the method, without repeating the earlier search for unpublished gauge code. [Primary paper, Methods and Appendix A/D](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26253v1#A1).

**A specified alternative exists:** Healy et al. give non-conformally-flat, Lorentz-boosted Schwarzschild CTT data: superpose attenuated backgrounds, remove the trace consistently and solve coupled elliptic equations for scalar `u` and vector `b^i`. Their standard and approximate branches differ; the latter retains nonzero constraint residuals. They also specify the approximate shock-avoiding lapse `f(alpha)=8/[3 alpha (3-alpha)]`, initial `alpha=1/(2 psi_BL-1)` and a Gamma-driver shift. Those published equations are an implementable research route, not a ready validated gamma22.366 configuration; their tabulated boosts reach about 4.13. [Healy et al., sections II A–C and Tables II/III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.06153).

The current TwoPuncturesC dependency constructs Bowen–York curvature and conformally flat spatial data; changing `solve_momentum_constraint` alone does not implement that CTT background. The current AthenaK lapse RHS uses a constant/linear-in-lapse coefficient multiplying `alpha*Khat`; it does not implement the rational function above. Porting the published gauge also requires resolving `K` versus `Khat=K-2Theta`, characteristic speeds, timestep control and the singularity at `alpha=3`. A silent clamp or arbitrary damping change would define a different method. [Pinned TwoPuncturesC equations](https://github.com/computationalrelativity/TwoPuncturesC/blob/ec563aeb672235b9443c330f9cde65f7246e8ea4/src/TP_Equations.c), [pinned metric construction](https://github.com/computationalrelativity/TwoPuncturesC/blob/ec563aeb672235b9443c330f9cde65f7246e8ea4/src/TwoPunctures.c#L696), [AthenaK lapse RHS](https://github.com/IAS-Astrophysics/athenak/blob/c5a0d7f9155a70149931bf0be5a4ffb673f2532a/src/z4c/z4c_calcrhs.cpp#L621).
